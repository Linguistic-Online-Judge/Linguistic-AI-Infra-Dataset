- Never split, merge, omit, normalize, or reorder tokens.

For ambiguous forms, prefer the analysis supported by surrounding morphology, agreement, dependencies, and clause structure.

Use these distinctions:

- ADP: prepositions, postpositions, and circumpositions governing nominal material. If the same form introduces a subordinate clause, use SCONJ instead.
- ADV: adverbial modifiers, including interrogative or relative forms used adverbially. Do not use ADV for grammatical particles when PART is appropriate.
- AUX: grammatical auxiliaries, copulas, and auxiliary-like modals under the relevant language's UD convention. Use VERB for lexical predicates, including lexical uses of otherwise auxiliary-looking forms.
- CCONJ: coordinators linking parallel words, phrases, or clauses.
- DET: articles and other adnominal determiners. Distinguish DET from a nominally functioning PRON; follow language-specific UD practice for possessives, demonstratives, interrogatives, and quantifiers.
- INTJ: interjections and standalone response or discourse forms when conventionally analyzed as interjections.
- NOUN: common nouns, including lexicalized or fully nominalized forms.
- NUM: cardinal numeral expressions and number tokens used numerically. Treat ordinals and numeral-like determiners according to their actual language-specific UD category.
- PART: grammatical particles that do not fit other closed classes, such as negation, infinitival, focus, aspect, or question particles where the language's UD analysis uses PART.
- PRON: forms functioning pronominally, including personal, reflexive, reciprocal, relative, interrogative, indefinite, or expletive pronouns as applicable.
- PROPN: proper-name words and name components functioning as proper nouns. Do not automatically tag capitalization as PROPN.
- PUNCT: punctuation marks and punctuation-like tokens, including sentence marks, brackets, and quotation marks.
- SCONJ: complementizers and subordinators that mark a dependent clause without filling a nominal or adverbial role inside it. Use PRON, DET, or ADV when a relative/interrogative form itself has such a role.
- SYM: symbols such as mathematical, currency, technical, or comparable non-punctuation symbols.
- VERB: lexical verbs, including infinitives, gerunds, and participles when they retain verbal function.

For contractions, clitics, multiword expressions, names, abbreviations, code-switching, and malformed text, tag each supplied token independently according to its contextual function while preserving the supplied tokenization.
