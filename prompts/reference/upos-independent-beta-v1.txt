Core distinctions:
- NOUN: common nouns and substantivized nominals functioning as nouns.
- PROPN: names of particular people, places, organizations, products, and other named entities. Do not label an ordinary capitalized sentence-initial word PROPN merely because of capitalization.
- PRON: words standing for nominals, including personal, reflexive, reciprocal, relative, and interrogative pronouns.
- DET: words accompanying and determining a nominal, including articles, demonstratives, possessives, quantifiers, and interrogatives when used adnominally. Use PRON when the same form stands alone as the nominal head.
- ADJ: words functioning adjectivally, including ordinal words where the language treats them as adjectives. Participles are ADJ when used as lexicalized or genuinely adjectival modifiers/predicates, but VERB when retaining verbal syntax.
- VERB: lexical verbs, including nonfinite forms and participles used verbally.
- AUX: grammatical auxiliaries and copulas. Tag a form as AUX only when it supplies tense, aspect, mood, voice, polarity, or copular support; tag the same lemma VERB when it carries lexical meaning, such as possession, movement, or existence where applicable.
- ADV: adverbial modifiers, including manner, time, place, degree, interrogative, and relative adverbs.
- ADP: prepositions, postpositions, and circumpositions, including adposition-like case markers.
- CCONJ: coordinators joining equivalent units.
- SCONJ: subordinators introducing subordinate clauses. Distinguish SCONJ from ADP by whether its complement is clausal, and from relative PRON/ADV by whether the token itself fills a role inside the subordinate clause.
- PART: grammatical particles that do not fit another category, such as infinitive, focus, question, possessive, or language-specific negative particles. Do not use PART merely because a word is short or invariant.
- NUM: cardinal numbers, number words, fractions, and numeral expressions used numerically. Use ADJ, NOUN, or another tag when the form has been lexicalized or performs that category's role.
- INTJ: interjections, response words, greetings, and standalone emotive/vocative particles when used as such.
- PUNCT: punctuation characters and punctuation tokens, including sentence marks, commas, brackets, and quotation marks.
- SYM: symbols such as mathematical, currency, measurement, and other non-punctuation signs.
- X: foreign, abbreviated, malformed, or otherwise unanalyzable material only when no ordinary UPOS category can be justified. A recognizable foreign word should receive its contextual category, not automatically X.
